Columns confirmed present on both tables in production.

**Refutation 1 — "the operator can just divide fee_cents ÷ amount_cents."** Fails in exactly the case the finding names. `stripe-capture-payment` rewrites `fee_cents = round(fullFee × pct)` while deliberately never rewriting `amount_cents` ("keep amount_cents as the originally-AUTHORIZED hold (audit record)"). So on a partial capture the rendered ratio is bps × pct, not bps — and a partial capture is what *creates* the dispute that brings the operator to this page. A 50% capture on a 700bps booking displays 3.5%, a rate that exists nowhere. It also fails when the Stripe floor binds: live row `amount_cents=500, fee_bps=1000` where `platform_fee_cents` returns 70 at *both* 700 and 1000 — two different pinned rates render an identical fee, making the rate not merely hard to derive but unrecoverable. And with a poster discount, capture computes the fee on `amount_cents + discountCents`, so `amount_cents` is already net of it and the ratio is wrong in a second direction.

**Refutation 2 — "Open in Stripe ↗ answers it."** No. `stripe-create-payment-intent/index.ts:390` writes metadata `{ booking_id, job_id, earner_id, poster_id }`. Stripe carries `amount` and `application_fee_amount` — the same two numbers already on screen — and no bps, credit or discount. The escape hatch reproduces the ambiguity rather than resolving it.

**Refutation 3 — "a control catches it."** Fee-related controls do exist (`poster_discount_underwater`, `discount_charged_not_delivered`, `earner_credit_missing`, `fee_tier_below_floor`, `promo_spend_understated`) — but they assert *system* consistency against data. None render the pinned rate to a human, and none fire when the system computed correctly and the operator simply cannot read what it computed. Their existence argues the data matters; it does not make it visible.

**Refutation 4 — "no real data exhibits this."** Refuted by production: 6 payments, **2 distinct `fee_bps` (700 and 1000)**, including two payments at the *identical* `amount_cents=2000` carrying different pinned rates ($2.00 vs $1.40 fee). The ambiguity is live today. A third source compounds it: `pin_booking_amount` returns early leaving `fee_bps_quoted` NULL when the job row/pay is missing, and `pin_payment_fee_bps` then defaults to 1000 — indistinguishable in the console from a genuine founding-rate booking (3 of 6 live rows have NULL `amount_cents_quoted`).

**Honest scope limit.** `fee_credit_cents` and `poster_discount_cents` are 0 across all 6 production payments, so the "did this consume a referral credit or poster discount?" half is a latent gap with no live instance yet. The `fee_bps` half is realized now. This is an observability/disclosure defect, not an exploitable vulnerability — no money moves wrong, and the pin's `service_role`-only UPDATE branch guarantees the stored values stay correct and immutable. The harm is human: an operator adjudicating a dispute or defending a chargeback must infer the rate, and the only inference available to them (today's rate card, or division) is wrong in precisely the partial-capture scenario that produced the dispute.

**Guard that stops it:** None. No guard, trigger, unique index, control or RLS policy prevents this, because none of them is a display surface. The relevant guards work in the opposite direction: `trg_z_pin_booking_amount` (via `pin_booking_amount`) and `trg_z_pin_payment_fee_bps` (via `pin_payment_fee_bps`) guarantee the four inputs are pinned at INSERT and immutable thereafter — the non-INSERT branch of `pin_booking_amount` restores `old.*` for any writer that is not `service_role`. So the correct values are reliably sitting in the row; the console just never selects or renders them. The fee-related controls (`poster_discount_underwater`, `discount_charged_not_delivered`, `earner_credit_missing`, `fee_tier_below_floor`, `promo_spend_understated`) detect system miscomputation against data, not an operator's inability to read a correct value. The two plausible non-console escape hatches both fail: PaymentIntent metadata carries only `{booking_id, job_id, earner_id, poster_id}` (no bps/credit/discount), and deriving the rate as fee_cents ÷ amount_cents is wrong whenever a partial capture, the Stripe-cost floor, a fee credit or a poster discount is in play — the first of which is the defining condition of the dispute that brings the operator to the page.
